## What is wrong with the current version
1. **The hero is a template hero.** Text on the left, a boxed photo on the right, a stamp badge, chips and two
   pill buttons. Premium references lead with big, immersive photography and bold type, not a split layout.
2. **On mobile the first screen has no photo at all**, just a wall of text and buttons.
3. **Too many small UI bits everywhere:** rating chips, monospace labels, numbered eyebrows, icon rows, tags and
   boxed cards in every section. It feels busy and app-like. Premium means fewer elements and more space.
4. **Everything is the same size and in a box.** Uniform 4-up cards, an evenly spaced 3x3 photo grid and rounded
   corners on everything. There is no scale contrast, no asymmetry and no full-bleed image.
5. **The colour is loud.** Bright crimson buttons on purple-charcoal look cheap. The headline in pink italic
   looks off-brand.
6. **The typography lacks drama.** The H1 is a long sentence set at one size, so it reads like a paragraph.
   Section headings are small, and body text sits in narrow columns surrounded by empty beige.
7. **The photos are too small.** The firm's best asset, its kitchen photography, is shown as thumbnails.

## What premium means here (do this)
- **Photography first.** At least one full-bleed (edge-to-edge) photo moment above the fold on desktop and on
  mobile. Use the best wide kitchen shot with a subtle warm gradient for legibility. Photos are large across the
  whole page: big feature images, staggered or overlapping compositions (like `pin527`, `pin143`, Interior ST),
  and a gallery with one or two hero-sized images and varied sizes, never a uniform grid of thumbnails.
- **Editorial typography with real scale contrast.** Use one display face (a refined high-contrast serif or a
  condensed caps grotesk, like Interior ST and `pin117`, `pin143`) and set key lines very large: about 72 to
  120px on desktop and 44 to 56px on phones. Pair it with a quiet sans for body text at 17 to 18px. Keep the full
  SEO H1 text, but style it as a short display line ("Kitchen Remodeling in Plymouth") with the rest of the
  sentence in a smaller second line. Use italic or script accents at most once or twice on the whole page.
- **Restraint.** Remove most chips, stamps, icon rows and monospace labels. Keep only what converts: the phone
  number, one primary button, the Google rating as one quiet line, and price and timeline facts as plain text.
  One accent colour, used sparingly.
- **Space and rhythm.** Generous section spacing (120 to 160px on desktop), wide margins and asymmetric
  layouts: text on a narrow column against a large image, offset headings, overlaps. Alternate a few strong
  bands (deep espresso, warm cream, a full-bleed photo band) instead of boxing every section.
- **Colour from the brand, used like a luxury brand would.** MJ's red is their brand colour. Keep it, but as a
  small accent only: the primary button, a thin rule, one detail. Build the base palette from warm neutrals taken
  from their own photos (cream, oak, stone, espresso). No bright crimson blocks and no pink headlines.
- **Fewer, stronger sections.** Merge or simplify where possible. For example, put the rating, licence and years
  in one elegant line, show the services as a large editorial list or alternating image rows instead of 4 small
  cards, and make the process a calm numbered list beside a big photo.
- **Details that feel crafted:** thin hairline rules, refined arrows, consistent photo crops (pick one radius or
  none, not rounded everything), smooth subtle fade-ins, and a large wordmark or closing statement in the footer.
